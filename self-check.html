Suspended or just unverified? A 10-point self-check

Not affiliated with Google. General information, not a guarantee. Google makes every decision about your Business Profile. This page summarizes Google's own help pages so you can check your listing yourself.

First: which problem do you have?

  • Suspended or disabled. Google says it may suspend or disable profiles that don't follow its guidelines. (Source: Google) In Google's appeals tool, you can see the decision, the reason, a link to the policy, and whether it can be appealed. (Source: Google)
  • Not verified. If the "Get verified" button shows up on your profile again, Google says it couldn't completely verify your business, and you should try to re-verify. (Source: Google)
  • Needs reverification or more info. If Google asks for more info to re-verify, Google says that's likely because some business details were recently updated. (Source: Google)
  • Account restricted. If your Google account is restricted, the profiles you manage are suspended. Google says to appeal the account restriction first, then the profile. (Source: Google)

The 10-point self-check

  1. Check the appeals tool first. It shows a status: Submitted, Approved, Not Approved, Can't be appealed, or Eligible for appeal. (Source: Google)
  2. Don't make a new profile. Google says not to create a new Business Profile for the same business. (Source: Google)
  3. Use your real business name only. Your name should match your real-world name on your signs, website, and paperwork. Google lists extras like marketing taglines as not permitted, and says details like address, service area, and category belong in their own sections, not the name. Google requires following its guidelines to keep a profile from being suspended. (Source: Google)
  4. Use a real address. P.O. boxes or mailboxes at remote locations aren't acceptable. A virtual office (a rented mailing address you don't work from) isn't eligible. (Source: Google)
  5. Service-area business? Check your address setting. If you go to customers and don't serve them at your address (for example, a plumber working from home), Google says to clear the address from your profile. If you also serve customers at a staffed location, you can show it. Your service area shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving from where you're based. For some businesses, larger service areas may be appropriate. (Source: Google)
  6. One profile per business. Google says there should be only one profile per business, and not more than one page for each location. Duplicates can cause problems. (Source: Google)
  7. Pick the fewest, most specific categories. Choose categories that finish the sentence "This business IS a...," not "has a." (Source: Google)
  8. Phone and website must be yours. The phone number must be under your business's direct control, with no redirects to other numbers or pages. (Source: Google)
  9. Gather documents that match. Google lists evidence that can help an appeal: official business registration, a business license, tax certificates, or utility bills for the business (electricity, phone, water, internet). The business name and address on them should match your profile. (Source: Google)
  10. You can file the appeal yourself. Use Google's appeals tool. Google says appeals can only be submitted once, so get your fixes and documents ready first. If the appeal is denied, Google may allow an additional review, where you can add evidence that wasn't in your first appeal. (Source: Google)

If Google asks you to verify

  • Google chooses the verification methods, and they can't be changed. (Source: Google)
  • For a video, Google requires a live, unedited recording of at least 30 seconds, made on a mobile device through your profile. Service-area businesses must show where they operate (street signs or landmarks at your business address; if you work from home, your immediate neighborhood), proof the business exists (tools, equipment, or branded items), and proof you run it (for example, unlocking a branded van, or a permit, invoice, or utility bill matching your profile name). Don't show bank, tax, or ID numbers, private info, or other people's faces. (Source: Google)
  • Google says video review can take up to 5 business days (sometimes longer), and if it isn't accepted, you can record a new one after reviewing the issues. (Source: Google)
